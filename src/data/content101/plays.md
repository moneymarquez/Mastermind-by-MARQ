# CONTENT 101 — THE PLAYS

**What this document is:** the tactical layer. Platform mechanics, content formats, production systems, and a specific section for Made by Marq. This decays — refresh roughly yearly.

**How to use it:** Fundamentals tells you why content spreads. This tells you what to make and where to put it. Never run a format from here without a defined viewer from Fundamentals Part 2.

**Last written:** 2026.

## PART 1 — PLATFORM PLAYBOOKS

### Instagram

**Best for:** local business, service providers, visual products, personal brand. The strongest platform for converting followers into local clients.

**Discovery:** Reels and the Explore page. Feed posts and Stories serve people who already follow you.

Mechanics:
- Reels are the growth engine. Everything else is retention.
- Vertical, full frame, no borders, no visible watermarks from other apps — reposted TikToks with watermarks are suppressed.
- Cover images matter because the grid is your profile. Consistent covers with readable text.
- Text on screen in the first frame does hook work before the audio loads — a large share of viewers start muted.
- Captions: first line is a second hook, it's the only part visible before "more."
- Carousels still perform well for teaching content and get saves, which are heavily weighted.
- Stories are for the existing audience: polls, questions, behind-the-scenes, and direct sales. Story engagement affects who sees your posts.
- Local hashtags and geotags genuinely help local discovery. Five to ten relevant tags, not thirty generic ones.
- DMs are the conversion channel. Sending a Reel to someone is the strongest signal on the platform.
- Collaborate feature: a post can appear on two accounts' feeds. Massively underused for cross-promotion with local businesses.

**Posting cadence:** 4–7 Reels/week for growth, plus Stories most days.

### TikTok

**Best for:** fast growth, testing hooks cheaply, younger and broader audience.

**Reality for a local service business:** reach is easier, conversion is harder, and the audience is geographically scattered. Excellent as a testing ground and a volume engine; weaker as a client source.

Mechanics:
- Native and unpolished wins. Production value actively hurts.
- Hook in the first second, not the first three.
- Distribution is genuinely per-post — a new account can hit big immediately.
- Text hook on screen from frame one.
- Trending audio gives a small boost; don't contort content to fit a trend.
- Comment section is a content source — reply with a video to good questions.
- Longer content (1–3 min) now performs if retention holds.
- Post 1–3× daily if you can produce it. Volume matters more here than anywhere.

**Best use:** test hooks and formats here at volume, move the winners to Instagram polished.

### YouTube Shorts

**Best for:** the same vertical content, second distribution, and permanence.

Mechanics: upload the same verticals. Shorts have a longer tail than TikTok or Reels — a video can pick up months later. Almost free to do since the content already exists. Strip watermarks.

### YouTube (long form)

**Best for:** trust-building, high-ticket sales, content that compounds indefinitely.

Reality: slow, high effort, and the single best channel for converting strangers into high-ticket clients. A video from two years ago still books calls. If capacity is limited, this comes after short-form is running.

### LinkedIn

**Best for:** B2B, which is what an agency is.

Mechanics:
- Personal profile massively outperforms company page.
- Text posts and short video. Native, no external links in the post body — links suppress reach; put them in a comment.
- Business owners and decision-makers are here in a buying mindset.
- Far less competition than Instagram. An underrated channel for agency client acquisition specifically.
- Post 3–5×/week. Written content, results, opinions, lessons.

### Facebook

**Best for:** reaching local business owners, who are disproportionately on Facebook and not on TikTok.

Mechanics:
- Local business groups and community groups are the play, not the feed.
- Participate genuinely; answer questions in your area of expertise. Direct promotion gets removed.
- A personal profile posting about work reaches local people better than a business page.
- Facebook Reels have weak competition and surprisingly strong local reach.

### Twitter/X

**Best for:** building in public, tech and business audiences, networking.
Weak for local client acquisition. Low priority unless you enjoy it.

## PART 2 — CONTENT FORMATS THAT WORK

These are formats, not ideas. Each can be filled with any subject.

### High-performing formats for service businesses

**1. The teardown**
Screen-record yourself analyzing a real business's marketing — their Google listing, their website, their ads. Point at specific problems. This is the highest-converting format for an agency because it demonstrates competence and cannot be faked.

**2. The fix, on camera**
Show the before, do the work, show the after. Screen recording or time-lapse. Proof content.

**3. The result**
"This client went from X to Y in Z time." Specific numbers, real screenshots. The single most valuable content you can make once you have results.

**4. The contrarian take**
"Stop posting every day." "Your website isn't the problem." Opinion drives comments, comments drive reach. Must be a stance you'll actually defend.

**5. The common mistake**
"Three things every food truck gets wrong on Google." Educational, shareable, and every viewer immediately self-diagnoses.

**6. The walkthrough**
Talking-head teaching one specific thing in under 60 seconds. Bread and butter. Not exciting alone; essential in volume.

**7. Behind the scenes**
The unglamorous reality. Working the day job, building at night, a call that went badly. This builds the parasocial connection that makes people buy from you rather than a bigger agency.

**8. The build in public**
Progress on a project, with numbers. "Month 1: 0 clients. Here's what I'm doing." Highly followable — it creates a reason to come back.

**9. Reply to a comment with a video**
Free content, guaranteed relevance, and the algorithm likes the engagement loop.

**10. The list**
"Five free things any local business can do today." Saves, which are weighted heavily.

**11. The green screen reaction**
React to a business's website, an ad, a news story. Fast to produce, easy to hook.

**12. The day in the life**
Works when the life is interesting or unusual. Ordinary days don't perform.

### Format rules

- Find 3–4 formats that work for you and run them into the ground. Repeatable format = fast production = volume.
- A format that worked once will work again. Nobody remembers.
- Every format needs a hook variant. The format is the body; the hook is separate work.

## PART 3 — THE PRODUCTION SYSTEM

The bottleneck is never ideas. It's production capacity. Build a system or this dies in three weeks.

### Weekly cycle

**Ideation (30 min, once a week)**
Pull from: client questions, cold call objections, comments, competitor content, things that annoyed you this week, problems you solved. Keep a running note. Never sit down to think of ideas from nothing.

**Hook writing (30 min)**
Write hooks before scripts. Write 20, keep 7. If the hook is weak, kill the idea — don't film it.

**Batch filming (90 min, once a week)**
One outfit change or several, same setup, film 7–10 videos back to back. This is the only sustainable model with a job.

**Editing (varies)**
Cut every dead moment. Captions burned in — most viewing is muted. Keep it fast.

**Scheduling**
Queue the week. Never post live daily; that's how consistency dies.

**Review (20 min, Sunday)**
Top 3, bottom 3, retention curves, follows per post, any DMs worth following up.

### Production rules

- Phone camera is fine. Lighting matters more than the camera — face a window.
- Audio matters more than video. A cheap lav mic is the highest-ROI purchase.
- One take, keep going, fix it in the edit.
- Don't restart because you stumbled — cut it.
- Have a default setup so filming has zero startup cost.

## PART 4 — THE FIRST 90 DAYS

**Days 1–7: Setup**
- Define the viewer in one sentence.
- Pick 3–5 pillars.
- Rebuild the profile: bio names audience and outcome, one link, clear photo.
- Write 30 hooks.
- Film the first batch of 10.

**Days 8–30: Volume**
- Post daily if possible, minimum 5×/week.
- Do not judge anything. You're building reps and teaching the algorithm who you are.
- Reply to every comment within an hour.
- Expect bad numbers. Everyone's are.

**Days 31–60: Pattern finding**
- Review weekly. Identify the 2–3 formats and topics that outperform.
- Cut what doesn't work. Double the frequency of what does.
- Pin the best three posts.
- Start converting: mention the DM, add a booking link.

**Days 61–90: Concentration**
- Run your winning formats almost exclusively.
- Repost the best performers with new hooks.
- Start the second platform with the same content.
- Begin measuring leads, not just followers.

**What normal looks like:** flat for 4–8 weeks, then movement. Most people quit at week 6.

## PART 5 — MADE BY MARQ: THE SPECIFIC PLAY

### The viewer

**Local small business owners — food trucks, restaurants, trades, salons, retail — who are busy but not growing, and who don't know what marketing to do.** Secondary: other young people trying to build something, who become an audience and occasionally a client.

Geography: Utah first, since local clients are the goal, then wider as the account grows.

### The positioning

The angle that separates you from every other marketing account: **you're 21, you built the software yourself, you work a day job, and you're doing this in public.** Almost every marketing account is a faceless tips page or a guru who's never run a local business. You're neither.

Two things to lead with:
1. **Proof over theory.** Show real teardowns and real results, not generic advice.
2. **The build in public.** Zero to an agency, on camera, with real numbers.

The second one is what makes people follow. The first is what makes them hire you.

### The pillars

**1. Teardowns (attract + convert)**
Analyze a real local business's marketing on screen. Their Google listing, their website, their Instagram. Name specific problems and specific fixes. Anonymize or ask permission.
*This is your best format. Do it more than anything else.*

**2. Free fixes (attract)**
"Three things every taco truck should do on Google today." Short, specific, immediately actionable. Shareable between business owners.

**3. Proof (convert)**
Every client result, with numbers and screenshots. You don't have these yet — which is why landing the first client matters more than the content plan.

**4. Build in public (retain)**
The honest version. Cold calls made, calls rejected, MRR, what you're building in Masterminds this week. Numbers make it followable.

**5. Contrarian (reach)**
"Your restaurant doesn't need a TikTok." "Stop paying for a website until you fix this." Stances local owners will argue about in the comments.

### Platform priority

1. **Instagram** — primary. Where local business owners are, best conversion to local clients.
2. **Facebook** — personal profile plus local business groups. Under-competed and full of your exact buyer.
3. **TikTok** — volume and hook testing. Repost winners to Instagram.
4. **LinkedIn** — text posts, B2B, low competition, real decision makers.
5. **YouTube Shorts** — free redistribution of the same content.

### Content that produces clients, ranked

1. Teardown of a business type you want to sell to (a food truck watching a food truck teardown is a lead)
2. A result with numbers
3. A free specific fix
4. A contrarian take that starts an argument in the comments
5. Build-in-public with real numbers

### The conversion path

Video → profile → pinned teardown → DM or booking link. Bio should say who you help and what happens, e.g. *"I get local businesses more customers. Utah. DM 'AUDIT' for a free teardown."*

**The free teardown offer is your lead magnet, and it's also your content.** Film the teardowns you give away. One piece of work, two outputs — a lead and a post.

### The honest sequencing note

Content is a slow lever. It will not produce a client this month. Cold calls will.

The right relationship between them: **calls produce clients, clients produce results, results produce the proof content that makes the account actually work.** An account with no results is just another tips page.

Run content as the compounding background asset. Run calls as the foreground. Do not let filming become a reason not to dial.

## PART 6 — DIAGNOSTIC SHORTCUTS

- **Views but no followers** → the content doesn't say why to follow, or the profile is unclear.
- **Followers but no leads** → no call to action, no proof, or the wrong audience.
- **Good hooks, low retention** → the body is slow. Cut harder.
- **Nothing works at all** → niche is blurry. The platform doesn't know who to show you to.
- **One video hit, nothing since** → make the same video again, five more ways.
- **Reach dropped suddenly** → usually a content shift that confused the profile, not a ban.
- **Local content, national audience** → add geographic markers, local references, geotags.
- **Comments but no DMs** → never asked.
- **Burned out at week 4** → volume was set for a good week. Lower it and hold it.
