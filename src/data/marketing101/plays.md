# MARKETING 101 — THE PLAYS

**What this document is:** the tactical layer. Specific plays, channel mechanics, and execution detail. This is the document that gets refreshed — roughly once a year, or whenever a platform materially changes.

**How to use it:** Fundamentals tells you *what to fix*. This tells you *how*. Never run a play from this document without first running the diagnosis in Fundamentals. A play applied to the wrong leak is wasted money.

**Last written:** 2026. Platform specifics decay; principles in Fundamentals do not.

---

## PART 1 — THE OPENING PLAYS

For almost any established local business, run these before spending a dollar on ads. They are free or near-free, fast, and they consistently outperform early paid spend.

### Play 1 — Google Business Profile overhaul

The highest-ROI free asset for a local business. Most are half-complete.

Checklist:
- Claim and verify. Correct primary category — this single field drives a large share of local ranking.
- Add every relevant secondary category.
- Complete every field: hours, holiday hours, service area, attributes, description, services list with prices.
- 20+ real photos, geotagged, added over time not all at once. Photos of the actual product, the actual people, the actual place.
- Post weekly. Offers, updates, events.
- Seed the Q&A section — you can ask and answer your own questions, and should.
- Respond to every review, positive and negative, within 48 hours.
- Add products/services with prices.
- UTM-tag the website link so you can see the traffic.

Expected impact: for a business starting from an unclaimed or thin profile, this alone can move call volume meaningfully within 30 days. Free.

### Play 2 — The review engine

Going from a low review count to a high one changes a business's economics permanently.

Mechanics:
- Ask every customer, at the moment of peak satisfaction — not days later.
- One tap. A short link or QR code straight to the review form. Every added step halves completion.
- Script it for staff so the ask is consistent.
- Text beats email for local service. Send within an hour of service.
- Never gate or filter reviews — it violates platform policy and it's transparently dishonest.
- Respond to all of them. Response rate is visible to prospects and to the algorithm.
- Target cadence: enough new reviews monthly that the recent ones are always recent. Stale reviews read as a dying business.

### Play 3 — Past-customer reactivation

The fastest revenue in marketing. Should be campaign one for nearly every established business.

Mechanics:
- Pull every past customer from whatever record exists — POS, phone, notebook, memory.
- Segment by recency: 0–90 days, 90–365, 365+.
- Message is personal, short, and gives a reason for the contact. Not a blast.
- A specific, time-bound offer for the 90–365 segment.
- Text outperforms email substantially for local. Email outperforms nothing.
- Expected: a meaningful percentage response from a list that cost nothing to build.

### Play 4 — The referral ask, structured

"Tell your friends" produces nothing. A structured ask produces referrals.

Mechanics:
- Ask at the moment of delight, not at checkout.
- Be specific about who: "Do you know anyone else on this street who's had the same problem?" Specificity triggers actual memory retrieval; vagueness triggers none.
- Make it easy to pass on — a card, a link, a forwardable text.
- Two-sided incentive works best: something for the referrer and the referred.
- Follow up on referrals within hours.

### Play 5 — Cross-promotion

Zero cost, immediate shared audience.

Mechanics:
- Identify businesses serving the same customer, non-competing. Taco truck and a brewery. Barber and a gym. Plumber and a realtor.
- Propose a specific trade: cards on each other's counters, a joint offer, a shared post, an event.
- The strongest version is a bundled offer that gives both audiences a reason to try the other.

### Play 6 — The missed-call audit

Before spending anything: call the business three times at different hours from an unknown number. Count how many go unanswered.

Fix, in order: someone answers, then a text-back-on-missed-call system, then online booking. Many local businesses lose more revenue here than any campaign could add.

## PART 2 — CHANNEL PLAYBOOKS

### Google Business Profile / Local Search

**Best for:** any business with a physical location or service area. Non-optional.
**Intent:** highest available. These people are ready.
**Cost:** free.
**Time to result:** 2–6 weeks.

Ranking drivers, roughly ordered: proximity, primary category, review quantity and velocity, profile completeness, posting activity, website relevance, citation consistency (NAP — name, address, phone identical everywhere).

### Google Search Ads

**Best for:** urgent, high-intent, high-ticket. Emergency services, trades, legal, medical.
**Intent:** very high.
**Budget floor:** roughly $500/month to learn anything in a competitive local market.
**Payback:** fast, often immediate.

Mechanics:
- Start with exact and phrase match. Broad match burns small budgets.
- Build the negative keyword list from day one and review it weekly. This is where most wasted spend lives.
- Bid on your own brand name if competitors are.
- Send traffic to a dedicated page matching the ad, not the homepage.
- Call extensions and call-only campaigns for emergency services.
- Location targeting tight — radius, not metro.

### Google Local Services Ads

**Best for:** licensed trades — plumbers, electricians, HVAC, locksmiths, cleaners.
**Why it matters:** pay per lead, not per click, and it sits above everything else on the page. Google Guaranteed badge.
**Requires:** license and insurance verification.

For a qualifying trade business, this generally outranks every other paid option. Check eligibility first, always.

### Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram)

**Best for:** discretionary purchases, visual products, local awareness, retargeting, lead generation for considered purchases.
**Intent:** low to moderate — you are creating demand, not capturing it.
**Budget floor:** roughly $300–500/month for a local radius campaign to exit the learning phase.

Mechanics:
- Creative is the dominant variable. Targeting matters far less than it used to; the algorithm finds the audience if the creative is right.
- Test 3–5 creatives per ad set, not 3–5 audiences.
- Video and native-looking content outperform polished ads for local business.
- Broad targeting with a tight geographic radius usually beats detailed interest targeting.
- Always run retargeting. Warm audiences convert at multiples of cold, and it's the cheapest inventory available.
- Build a custom audience from the customer list and a lookalike from it.
- Conversion tracking installed before launch, not after.
- Give it 7 days and enough conversions before judging.

### Instagram Organic

**Best for:** visual products, food, personal services, anything where seeing it triggers wanting it.
**Time to result:** slow. Months, not weeks. Do not sell this as a fast lever.

Mechanics:
- Reels are the discovery mechanism. Feed posts and stories serve existing followers.
- Post consistently over posting frequently. Three a week forever beats daily for two weeks.
- The first 1–2 seconds decide everything.
- Local hashtags and geotags matter for local discovery.
- Reply to every comment and DM fast.
- The bio must state what, where, and one action.
- Behind-the-scenes and process content consistently outperforms polished product shots for small local businesses.

### TikTok

**Best for:** visual, entertaining, younger audience, food, transformation content.
**Not for:** older demographics, emergency services, most B2B, anything where the customer is 55+.

Mechanics:
- Native, unpolished, fast. Production value hurts here.
- Hook in the first second.
- Trends give reach but only convert if the product fits.
- Local reach is weaker than Instagram's for a service business.
- Volume matters more than on other platforms — the algorithm gives every post a chance.

### Email

**Best for:** everyone. The most underused owned channel in small business.
**Cost:** near zero.

Mechanics:
- Build the list from day one. Every transaction is a capture point.
- Segment by behavior — recency, purchase type, value.
- Consistency beats cleverness. A simple, regular email outperforms a beautiful sporadic one.
- Plain-text style often outperforms designed templates for small local businesses.
- Subject line does 80% of the work.
- One CTA per email.
- Reactivation sequences and abandoned-anything sequences produce the highest return.

### SMS

**Best for:** local service, appointments, time-sensitive offers, reactivation.
**Open rates:** far above email. Use accordingly and sparingly.

Mechanics:
- Explicit opt-in required. Compliance matters — TCPA violations are expensive.
- Short, personal, one action.
- Never more than a few a month unless transactional.
- Best uses: appointment reminders, missed-call text-back, reactivation, limited-time local offers.

### Nextdoor

**Best for:** home services, local trades, childcare, anything neighborhood-trust-driven.
**Underrated.** Recommendation threads on Nextdoor drive real volume for trades and are almost entirely ignored by marketers.

Mechanics: claim the business page, participate genuinely as a neighbor, respond to recommendation requests. Paid options exist but organic participation outperforms.

### Yelp

**Best for:** restaurants, food, some services.
**Reality:** declining but still meaningful in some categories and regions. Claim it, keep it accurate, respond to reviews. Be cautious with their sales calls — the paid product rarely pays back for small operators.

### YouTube / Shorts

**Best for:** anything requiring explanation, demonstration, or trust-building before purchase. High-ticket services.
**Time to result:** slow, but content compounds indefinitely — a video from three years ago still brings customers, unlike any paid ad.

### Pinterest

**Best for:** home, food, weddings, beauty, design, anything planned in advance.
**Underrated for:** contractors, event services, bakeries. Long content lifespan.

### LinkedIn

**Best for:** B2B, professional services, high-ticket, recruiting.
**Not for:** consumer local business.
The owner's personal profile outperforms the company page substantially.

### Reddit

**Best for:** niche communities, local city subreddits, technical products.
**Rules:** self-promotion is punished. Participate genuinely or don't participate. Local city subreddits can drive real traffic for a distinctive local business, but only through authentic presence.

## PART 3 — OFFLINE AND UNCONVENTIONAL PLAYS

Systematically overlooked, frequently more effective per dollar than digital for local businesses. Never treat these as second-tier.

### Direct mail / EDDM

**Why it works now:** mailboxes are empty and inboxes are full. Response rates for well-executed local direct mail have held up while digital costs climbed.

Mechanics:
- Every Door Direct Mail lets you target by postal route without a list — cheap and genuinely effective for local.
- Oversized postcards outperform letters for local service.
- One offer, one deadline, one action.
- A handwritten-look envelope outperforms a designed mailer for high-ticket.
- Repetition matters — one drop is a test, three drops is a campaign.

### Door hangers and yard signs

For trades: a yard sign at every job site, and door hangers on the surrounding houses the day you're working. Proximity plus proof plus timing. Extremely cheap, consistently effective, almost never done systematically.

### Vehicle wrap

A work truck is a permanent billboard driving through the exact service area. One-time cost, years of impressions. Must include one large, readable phone number and one clear statement of what they do.

### Flyers, posters, community boards

Laundromats, coffee shops, libraries, grocery stores, gyms, churches, community centers. Free, hyper-local, and effective for services with an older or lower-online-engagement customer base.

### Church and school bulletins

Cheap, trusted, and reaches an audience most competitors don't touch. Strong for trades, food, and family services.

### Local radio

Still meaningful for reaching drivers, older demographics, and rural markets. Frequency matters more than reach — a small buy repeated beats a large buy once.

### Local print / free papers / penny savers

Written off by most marketers, which is exactly why they can work. Strong for older demographics and small towns. Cheap.

### Events, festivals, farmers markets

For food, retail, and consumer services: direct sampling plus list capture plus immediate revenue. Every event should have a capture mechanism — QR to a list, a giveaway entry, a punch card.

### Sponsorship

Local sports teams, school events, community causes. Bought for goodwill, but works because of repeated local visibility to families with money.

### Employer partnerships

For food: catering deals with local offices, hospitals, and warehouses. One relationship can outweigh months of ads. Almost nobody pitches this directly.

### Sampling at high-traffic locations

Give the product away where the customer already is. Expensive per unit, high conversion, immediate.

### Loyalty punch cards

Crude and effective. Increases frequency, which for a food business is the entire game.

### Handwritten notes

To past customers, after a large job, at a holiday. Almost extinct, therefore memorable. Very high response per unit sent for high-ticket services.

### Local press pitch

A genuinely interesting angle — a milestone, a community act, a unique product — can produce coverage worth more than any ad, at zero cost. Local outlets need content constantly.

### Local influencer / food account partnerships

For food and consumer businesses: a local account with 10k engaged local followers outperforms a national account with a million. Usually costs a free meal.

## PART 4 — PLAYS BY BUSINESS TYPE

### Local food — truck, restaurant, catering

Priority order: Google Business Profile → reviews → Instagram organic with real food video → local food accounts → catering/employer outreach → events → loyalty → local Facebook groups.

Notes: catering is where the margin is; most food businesses under-market it. Location posting is critical for trucks — a daily "where we are today" habit builds a following that follows. Photos of actual food beat everything.

### Home services and trades — plumber, electrician, HVAC

Priority order: Google Local Services Ads → Google Business Profile → Google Search → review engine → yard signs and door hangers → Nextdoor → past-customer maintenance reminders.

Notes: emergency intent lives on search, not social. Response speed is the whole ballgame. Maintenance reminder sequences to past customers are nearly free recurring revenue and almost nobody does them.

### Personal services — salon, barber, gym

Priority order: Instagram organic (before/after is the native content) → Google Business Profile → reviews → referral program → rebooking at checkout → local partnerships.

Notes: retention is everything; the constraint is rebooking rate, not new leads. Individual stylist/trainer accounts often outperform the business account.

### Retail

Priority order: Google Business Profile → Instagram → local events → email list → community partnerships → Facebook Marketplace.

### Professional services — B2B, consulting, agency

Priority order: positioning and niche → LinkedIn personal profile → direct outreach → case studies and proof → referral network → email list.

Notes: one strong case study outperforms a year of posting.

### Events and weddings

Priority order: Pinterest → Instagram → vendor referral network → The Knot-style directories → past-client referrals.

Notes: the vendor network is the channel. Photographers, planners, and venues refer each other constantly.

## PART 5 — THE CAMPAIGN LAUNCH SEQUENCE

1. Baseline recorded — current leads, revenue, traffic, review count.
2. Tracking installed and verified before anything launches.
3. Offer finalized and stress-tested against the four components.
4. Conversion path fixed — page, phone, booking, response time.
5. Free plays running.
6. One channel selected, with reasoning.
7. Creative built: 3–5 variants minimum.
8. Small launch with a defined test budget and duration.
9. Do not touch it for the defined period.
10. Read the result against the baseline, one variable.
11. Kill or scale.
12. Capture the winner into the swipe file with its numbers.

## PART 6 — DIAGNOSTIC SHORTCUTS

Fast reads for common situations:

- **Busy but broke** → pricing, not marketing.
- **Good reviews, no volume** → awareness or Google Business Profile.
- **Traffic, no calls** → conversion or offer.
- **Calls, no bookings** → sales process or price positioning.
- **One-time customers only** → retention, no list.
- **Competing on price constantly** → positioning.
- **Ads worked then stopped** → creative fatigue, or the offer was seasonal.
- **Great product, no growth** → nobody knows, or nobody's asked for referrals.
- **Leads that never close** → response speed. Check it first.
- **Seasonal collapse** → they need a second offer for the off-season, not more ads.
